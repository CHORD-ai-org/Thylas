Section 6Integrated framework

The framework below is not a model of the learner or an algorithm. It is a statement of which variables the participation decision depends on, which theory supplies each variable, and how confident we can be that the variable matters.

6.1 Which theory answers which question

The five clusters in Section 3 are often treated as competing accounts of the same thing. They are not. Each answers a different question, and the framework only works if they are kept in their lanes.

For Lemma this means the AI does not need to represent the problem the way the learner does; it needs a representation that supports the same inferences at the points where they must coordinate.

6.2 The decision is five dimensions, not three states

ASK, OFFER and STAY QUIET are projections of a higher-dimensional decision onto one axis. Treated as a three-way classifier, they force choices that the theory says should be independent: an offer can be delivered as a question, a question can be delivered silently as a workspace annotation, and silence can be either invisible or marked. The decomposition below separates what the literature actually treats separately.

Table 9. Five dimensions of a participation act

The three product modes survive as familiar labels for common combinations — ASK is typically D2 = challenge or clarify at D3 = question; OFFER is D2 = supply or mark at D3 = single line; QUIET is D3 = zero. The advantage of the decomposition is that it makes the design falsifiable: a failed interaction can be attributed to timing, function, intensity, channel or persistence, rather than to "the wrong mode".

6.4 Two structural problems the framework exposes

The accounting asymmetry

False interventions and false silences are not symmetric in a product. An unnecessary interruption is immediate, attributable and often complained about; a missed opportunity for support is invisible, because the learner simply continues with a worse plan and no one observes the counterfactual. The measurement literature makes this worse: in-session performance rises with assistance [[koed2007,bastani2025]], so the metric that teams watch moves in the direction of more intervention. Nothing in the literature corrects for this, and it is the single most likely reason a well-intentioned selective system drifts toward an always-on assistant.

The legibility problem

Grounding requires evidence of understanding, and in a human pair that evidence is produced continuously by both parties [[clarkbrennan1991]]. An AI that stays quiet produces no evidence of anything. The learner therefore cannot distinguish three states that require different responses: the AI understood and chose not to intervene; the AI did not understand the reference; the AI is not attending. Klein's observability requirement is the general form of the fix [[klein2004]], and Amershi's guidance on making system state clear is the product form [[amershi2019]]. The specific implication for Lemma is stronger than either: silence must be rendered as a state, not as an absence.

Nardi's argument that mediated communication is about connection rather than bandwidth is relevant to how that state should be rendered [[nardi2005]]. Connection — the sense that a partner is present, attending, and committed — is not carried by information volume. A minimal, continuously visible indication of attention may do more for the learner's willingness to rely on the system than any amount of spoken explanation.

6.5 What this framework is not

It is not an algorithm, and the literature does not support building one yet. Four components would be required, and each is currently the weakest link in its own area: a calibrated estimate of the learner's automated repertoire (expertise reversal tells us this matters, not how to measure it); a detector of learning-relevant boundaries in open-ended work (interruption research assumes the boundaries exist); a model of learning value per act type (the assistance dilemma says this is the crux and supplies no function); and a policy that can be evaluated on delayed outcomes (almost no study in this corpus does this). Writing a decision rule now would encode guesses as if they were findings.

Section 7Critical evaluation of product hypotheses

Each hypothesis is assessed on three questions: what supports it, what contradicts it, and what observation would change the verdict. The last question is the one that makes the assessment useful.

Table 11. Summary of verdicts
HypothesisVerdictDecisive consideration

H2 Workspace groundingPartialCost model is strong; the AI side of grounding is unverified and can fail silently.
H3 Mixed initiativePartialStructure is sound; learning-specific evidence and interruption costs pull against it.
H4 Learner agencyConflictingControl is valuable in principle; learners regulate help-seeking poorly in practice.
H5 Long-term continuityInsufficientNo longitudinal learning evidence; the plausible mechanism differs from the claimed one.
H6 Voice as coordinationInsufficientNo direct test; component evidence points both ways.

H1 — Selective participation

Claim. An AI that deliberately chooses when not to intervene may support learning better than an AI that responds to every opportunity.

Support. The components are among the best-established findings in the corpus. Assistance that replaces generative work raises performance and lowers learning [[koed2007,soder2015]]; the same support reverses sign with expertise [[kalyuga2003]]; generation attempts before instruction improve later learning [[kapur2008]]; and in the LLM era, unconstrained access produced a 17% decline in unaided performance while a safeguarded tutor largely avoided it [[bastani2025]]. The contrast between the safeguarded and unconstrained conditions in that experiment is the closest thing in the literature to a test of "selection matters".

Against. The hypothesis compares selective participation to "responding to every opportunity", but the realistic alternative is "responding when asked" — and that baseline fails for a different reason: learners do not ask well [[aleven2003]]. A system that withholds intervention inherits that failure. VanLehn's plateau finding also warns that richer, better-timed interaction does not reliably produce larger gains than competent step-level interaction [[vanlehn2011]], so the burden of proof is on the added complexity.

Verdict: partial support. The mechanisms are supported; the comparative claim is not. Nothing in the corpus shows that an AI's decision to stay silent improves learning relative to a well-designed learner-initiated system.

What would settle it. A three-arm randomised trial — always-answer, selective, learner-initiated-only — with identical content and a delayed transfer test. The mediator to measure is the learner's own help-seeking behaviour, because the mechanism claim is that selection trains better regulation, not merely that it reduces interruption.

H2 — Workspace grounding

Claim. Grounding spoken interaction in the learner's current workspace may reduce context reconstruction and improve collaboration quality.

Support. Grounding is cost-sensitive, and shared visual access removes the largest cost — describing referents [[clarkbrennan1991]]. Reference converges through iterative repair under least collaborative effort, which is only cheap when both parties can see the object [[clarkwg1986]]. Visual information about the objects of work is more valuable than visual information about the participants, and disjoint perspectives undermine communication [[whittaker2003]]. External representations change the task itself, so the workspace is not neutral context [[zhang1994]].

Against. All of this evidence is human–human, and it assumes symmetrical access to evidence of understanding. The AI's grounding is one-sided: it can ground its utterance in the workspace, but it cannot verify that the learner has understood, and a confidently wrong resolution is worse than an explicit question because it is invisible until it produces a bad contribution. Distributed cognition's unit of analysis also cuts the other way: if the workspace is part of the cognitive system, then an AI reading the workspace is reading the learner's thinking, which raises a consent question the literature does not address [[hollan2000]].

Verdict: partial support. The cost model transfers; the mechanism by which an AI achieves grounding does not, and its failure mode is silent.

What would settle it. A within-subject comparison of the same AI policy with and without workspace access, measuring reference-resolution accuracy, the rate at which the learner must restate context, and the frequency of confidently wrong resolutions. "Context reconstruction" needs an operational definition — time-to-first-productive-move after a session break is a candidate.

H3 — Mixed initiative

Claim. Allowing both learner and AI to initiate may support more adaptive collaboration than purely user-initiated chat.

Support. Mixed initiative has a formal decision structure [[horvitz1999]], a lineage in tutoring systems that predates chat [[carbonell1970]], and a control vocabulary that separates type from level [[para2000]]. Team-player requirements specify what an initiating agent owes its partner: directability, observability, predictability [[klein2004]]. Product guidelines converge on the same commitments [[amershi2019]].

Against. Proactive behaviour is the most robustly costly thing an interface can do. Timing alone changes performance and social attribution [[adamczyk2004]]; resumption cost scales with the interruption's demand [[monk2008]]; residue persists after return [[leroy2009]]. Automation research documents the downstream failures of initiative: complacency, over-trust, and loss of situation awareness when the human must take over [[parariley1997,endsley1995]]. Users of conversational agents report that unprompted agent behaviour is one of the main sources of disappointment [[luger2016]], and the direct-manipulation critique predicts exactly this — delegation moves monitoring cost onto the user [[shneimaes1997]].

Verdict: partial support. The architecture is well motivated and the risks are well documented; what is missing is any learning-outcome evidence. Every empirical study cited here measured task performance, affect, or attitudes — not retention or transfer.

What would settle it. The same three-arm design as H1, with an added process measure: does AI-initiated interaction increase or decrease the learner's own initiation over time? A mixed-initiative system that progressively silences the learner has failed on its own terms.

H4 — Learner agency

Claim. Giving learners meaningful control over accepting, rejecting and redirecting AI participation may help preserve their agency and independent reasoning.

Support. Control is a design obligation in the human–AI guidelines (efficient invocation and dismissal) [[amershi2019]] and in the automation ladder, where human veto is a level, not an afterthought [[para2000]]. Directability is a defining property of a team player [[klein2004]]. Social-response findings suggest that explicit, low-cost rejection is important precisely because rejecting a spoken agent carries social weight [[nass2000]].

Against. This is the hypothesis with genuinely conflicting evidence rather than merely thin evidence. Learners do not regulate help-seeking well: they avoid help that threatens self-esteem, request answers instead of principles, and misjudge when they need support [[aleven2003]]. Help-seeking skill is trainable, which implies it is a capacity, not a given [[roll2011]]. Worse, control without understanding can be illusory: operators given control at high automation levels still lost situation awareness [[endsley1995]], and the direct-manipulation debate turns on whether control actually restores comprehension or only the feeling of it [[shneimaes1997]].

Verdict: conflicting evidence. The hypothesis is defensible as a value commitment, but as an empirical claim about learning it is unresolved and has a serious counterargument.

The distinction that resolves the conflict. "The learner can veto" and "the learner must initiate" are different designs, and the evidence supports the first while undermining the second. A system with proactive but fully rejectable participation preserves agency without inheriting the under-helping bias. This distinction should be written into the product definition, because the current phrasing of H4 admits both readings.

What would settle it. A 2×2 design crossing who initiates with who can veto, measuring learning, voluntary help-seeking, and a validated agency or ownership measure — plus a manipulation check that learners can actually predict what the AI will do.

H5 — Long-term continuity

Claim. Maintaining relevant context across sessions may improve sustained learning and collaborative problem solving.

Support. Common ground is the limiting resource in sustained collaboration, and distance matters because maintaining it is expensive [[olson2000]]. Transactive memory systems — specialisation, credibility, coordination — predict team performance [[lewis2003]]. Persistent records support talk about objects over time, and speech alone is often sufficient when the record is shared [[whittaker2003]]. Articulation work is real labour that a partner can absorb [[schmidt1992]].

Against. Collaborative memory is not simply additive: groups exhibit retrieval disruption and collaborative inhibition, and a shared record can anchor participants to an earlier, wrong framing [[rajaram2010]]. Accumulated context also produces false confidence in shared understanding, which Olson's framework treats as a failure mode rather than a benefit [[olson2000]]. And the mechanism Lemma's hypothesis implies — memory enables personalisation enables learning — is not the mechanism the evidence supports. The supported mechanism is narrower: memory reduces the cost of re-establishing common ground.

Verdict: insufficient evidence. Theoretically coherent, empirically unestablished for AI partners, and with a credible harm pathway that the product currently does not measure.

What would settle it. A crossover study over several weeks in which the AI's carried-forward context is (a) full, (b) absent, (c) present but learner-reviewable and correctable. Outcomes: time to re-ground at session start, delayed retention, and the rate at which the learner corrects the AI's representation of prior work.

H6 — Voice as coordination

Claim. Voice may be especially valuable as a low-friction coordination channel during ongoing cognitive activity, rather than as a replacement for text.

Support. The modality effect shows a reliable advantage for spoken text accompanying visual material [[ginns2005]]; multiple-resource theory explains why speech costs little when visual and manual resources are occupied [[wickens2002]]; and users spontaneously assign descriptive content to speech and spatial content to pen when both are available [[cohen1992,oviatt1997]]. Connection, not bandwidth, is what a communication channel supplies [[nardi2005]].

Against. The modality advantage reverses when spoken content is long and transient [[leahy2011,singh2012]] — and coordination utterances are short, which helps, but the same literature says the boundary is re-inspectability, not length. Users' experience of conversational agents is dominated by disappointment relative to the conversational promise [[luger2016]], and speech in shared physical space is a public, accountable act [[porcheron2018]]. Oviatt's myths paper warns specifically against assuming that speech is the natural or preferred modality [[oviatt1999]], and Clark's analysis shows that conversational form is often not what users want for instrumental tasks [[clark2019]].

Verdict: insufficient evidence. Not contradicted, but untested. Every supporting finding concerns either spoken instructional output or input efficiency in a multimodal desktop task. None concerns voice as a coordination layer over a shared learning workspace, which is the actual claim.

What would settle it. A within-subject experiment holding the AI policy constant and varying only channel: voice, inline text, and voice-with-deixis. Measure coordination latency (time from intention to successful redirection), repair rate, secondary-task performance as a load proxy, and delayed learning. The prediction from the theory is specific: voice should win on coordination latency and lose on content-heavy acts — if it does not, the coordination thesis is wrong for a reason worth knowing.

Section 8Unresolved questions

These are questions the literature does not answer and that Lemma cannot avoid answering in design. They are stated so that they can be investigated rather than assumed.

  1. Does silence have a learning benefit, or only a comfort benefit? No study isolates "the AI decided not to intervene" from "the AI was absent". The productive-failure evidence concerns sequence, not presence, so the benefit of a watching-but-quiet partner is inferred rather than shown.
  2. Can a learning-relevant boundary be detected in open-ended work? All breakpoint research assumes a task grammar. Mathematical exploration and research have soft boundaries; whether pause-and-edit patterns are a valid proxy is unknown.
  3. Is a proactive question better than a proactive explanation at equal information? Chi's framework predicts yes [[chi2009]]; the assistance dilemma predicts the answer depends on expertise [[koed2007]]. Neither has been tested with a language-model partner.
  4. Does voice interfere with the learner's own verbal processing? The mechanism is plausible from resource theory [[wickens2002]], and articulatory suppression is well documented, but no study measures the effect of an AI's speech on a learner's inner speech during reading or reasoning.
  5. What is the right granularity of participation control? Per act, per session, per task type, per learner? Guidelines recommend dismissal [[amershi2019]]; nothing establishes whether learners want to configure participation or merely to veto it.
  6. Does persistent context improve re-grounding or entrench a framing? Both are predicted by different parts of the same literature [[olson2000,rajaram2010]], and the sign may depend on whether the learner can inspect and correct what is carried forward.
  7. How should the AI display uncertainty without adding noise? Explanation requirements [[amershi2019]] and observability [[klein2004]] both imply visibility; neither says how often a confidence signal can be shown before it becomes another interruption.
  8. Is "co-regulation" with a non-regulating agent a coherent construct? Regulation theory assumes a partner with its own goals and self-regulation [[jarvela2013]]. If the AI has neither, what Lemma calls co-regulation may be better described as contingent tool use — which would change what should be measured.

Section 10Implications for Lemma AI

Ten principles, each stated as a design commitment with the theory behind it and the observation that would falsify it. These are derived from the review, not retrofitted to existing decisions — several of them argue against features the product currently assumes.

10.1 What follows for the three modes

AI ASKS. Questions are the highest-value, highest-cost act: they preserve generation [[chi2009]] and they demand a reply, which extends the interruption [[monk2008]]. They should therefore be rare, short, and placed at boundaries. The design question is not "what should the AI ask?" but "what is the smallest question that changes what the learner does next?"

AI OFFERS. Offers are cheaper because they do not require uptake, but they are also easier to ignore and easier to over-produce. Their natural form is not a sentence in a chat log but a mark on the object — closer to an annotation than to a message, which also makes the offer re-inspectable and removes the transience penalty [[leahy2011]].

AI STAYS QUIET. This is the mode that most needs engineering. As specified, it is indistinguishable from a system that is confused or absent. Rendering it as an observable state is the minimum condition for it to function as a collaboration signal, and the cost of getting this wrong is that the learner attributes the system's silence to their own incomprehensibility — a failure mode with no instrumentation.

10.2 What the evidence does not let Lemma claim

  • That voice improves learning. No study supports this in a shared workspace, and the transience evidence predicts specific harms for long spoken content.
  • That silence improves learning. The mechanism is plausible and the comparative test has not been run.
  • That proactive intervention is better than learner-initiated help. The strongest direct evidence supports constrained assistance, not AI-initiated assistance [[kestin2025,bastani2025]].
  • That long-term memory of a learner improves outcomes. The mechanism the evidence supports is cheaper re-grounding, which is a different and smaller claim.
  • That conversation is the wrong container for collaboration. This is a hypothesis about interaction design, and the evidence is consistent with it without establishing it.

10.3 Instrumentation this implies

Three measurements follow directly from the review and are not currently standard in AI tutoring products. First, a silence ledger: every decision not to act, with its context, sampled and coded for missed opportunity — without it, the accounting asymmetry in Section 6.4 guarantees drift. Second, a re-grounding measure: time from session start to the learner's first productive move, which is the operational form of the H2 and H5 claims. Third, paired outcome measurement: every in-session performance metric accompanied by a delayed or transfer measure, because the two are known to diverge [[soder2015]].
