Note · Research

Method — how we measure comparability

Ceaser Zhao · edited yesterday 18:40 · 6 min read · 3 references

Two runs are comparable when the prompt set, the tokenizer, and the normalization are the same. If any of the three changes, the number changes, and we are no longer measuring the model.

1 · The problem

Our earlier baselines were reported per item. When the tokenizer changed, the item count stayed the same but the token count did not, so a lower score could mean a worse model or a shorter denominator.[1]

The denominator is part of the measurement. Changing it silently is changing the claim.

2 · What we do instead

  • Record the tokenizer version in the run manifest, always.
  • Normalize per 1,000 tokens, never per item.
  • Report Δ with a 95% interval; a point estimate alone is not a result.
  • Re-run the previous tokenizer as a control whenever it changes.

3 · Open

Wu 2025 reports a 6–11% swing, but two of three replications failed once prompts were fixed. We do not yet know which of the two effects dominates. The control run in baseline-sweep is meant to answer that.

References

[1] Wu et al. 2025 — wu-2025.pdf
[2] Protocol v3, §3 — protocol-v3.pdf
[3] baseline-sweep, run #12 — process
